      1. PREP Agents: TDF/emtricitabine daily (others: TAF/emtricitabine, cabotegravir every 2 months)
      2. On-demand prep: how to take? 2 doses 2-24 hrs. before sex, 1 dose 24 hours after first dose, and 1 dose 48 hours after first dose
        1. FDA approved?? No, and not recommended by CDC at this time
      3. TasP (treatment as prevention)
        1. Goal HIV RNA: < 200; other forms of prevention should be used during the first 6 months of treatment until HIV RNA < 200
      4. Prevention of maternal fetal transmission
        1. Pregnant women with HIV NOT ON ART
          1. Preferred regimens: dual NRTI (ABC/LAM or tenofovir/emtricitabine) + ritonavir boosted PI or INSTI
          2. How long to continue combo regimen - through intrapartum period
        2. Pregnant women with HIV RECEIVING ART
          1. Continue current combo regimen if already receiving therapy, through intrapartum period
  • Pregnant women with HIV IN LABOR (with or without therapy)
    1. IV zidovudine if HIV RNA > 1000 or unknown HIV RNA; however, this is not necessary for HIV infected women with HIV RNA < 50 during late pregnancy
  1. Infants born to mothers who are HIV positive
    1. Low risk of HIV transmission to baby treatment and how long: zidovudine for 4 weeks
    2. High risk of HIV transmission to baby treatment and how long: presumptive HIV therapy for 6 weeks (zidovudine, lamivudine and nevirapine, OR zidovudine lamivudine and raltegravir)
  2. PEP
    1. Nonoccupational – begin prophylaxis within 72 hrs and treat for 4 weeks; regimen – RTG or DTG + TDF/emtricitabine daily.
    2. Occupational – begin prophylaxis within hours; even if HIV status of course of patient is unknown; administer for 4 weeks; regimen – RTG BID or RTG HD or DTG plus TDF/emtricitabine or lamivudine.
  3. Treatment of patients who is HIV positive
    1. Start HIV treatment regardless of cd4 count
    2. Recommended initial regimen for treatment naïve – INSTI BOOSTED REGIMEN
  • Only use abacavir for patients who are HLAB5701 negative
  1. Only use rilpivirine or raltegravir if its only part of a 2 drug regimen for pts with VL (viral load, HIV RNA) < 100,000 and CD4 > 200
  2. Avoid TDF in patients with CrCl < 70
  1. Regimens to consider when ABC, TAF and TDF cannot be used
    1. DTG + LAM
    2. DRV/r + RAL
  • DRV/r + LAM
  1. Initiating potent combo ART in the setting of acute opportunistic infections
    1. Opportunistic infections with no effective therapy require prompt initiation of ART: cryptosporidiosis, microsporidiosis, promyelocytic leukemia, and Kaposi sarcoma.
    2. ART should begin within a certain amount of weeks for acute opportunistic infections
      1. See below:
        1. Within 2 weeks when CD4 < 50: TB
        2. Within 8-12 weeks for all others
        3. After 5 weeks for cryptococcal meningitis
      2. PJP
        1. Treatment:
          1. Preferred: TMP/SMX (IV for moderate-severe) or PO (mild-moderate)
          2. Others: clinda/primaquine, pentamidine, TMP/dapsone, atovaquone
  • Adjuvant therapy: corticosteroids; recommended for severe (A-a gradient > 35 or PO2 <70 mm Hg) PJP; start within 72 hrs
    1. Use cautiously in patients with TB
  1. All these agents also have prophylactic dosing FYI
  2. Primaquine and dapsone (and TMP at low doses) are associated with methemoglobinemia, test for G6PD deficiency
  3. Atovaquone must be taken with a high-fat meal; in terms of PJP, this is only indicate for mild-moderate severity
  • Atovaquone has potential for decreased efficacy in pts with diarrhea 2/2 poor absorption
  1. Prophylaxis
    1. Can be discontinued when? CD4 > 200 for at least 3 months on ART
  2. Toxoplasmosis
    1. Hosts: felines ☹ (change litter box daily, wash hands after changing litter box, or have someone else change the litter box, and keep the cat indoors)
    2. Diagnosis: brain biopsy the only definitive diagnosis; also antibodies or T. gondii isolation in serum or CSF; MRI showing brain lesions
    3. Treatment
      1. Pyrimethamine + sulfadiazine
        1. Add leucovorin to reduce bone marrow suppressive effects of pyrimethamine
      2. Watch out for crystalluria – sulfadiazine
    4. Prophylaxis
      1. When to use prophylaxis – toxoplasma-seropositive patients with CD4 < 100
      2. Primary Regimen: Bactrim once daily (or dapsone/pyrimethamine/leucovorin or atovaquone +/- pyrimethamine)
        1. When to stop prophylaxis: CD4 > 200 for at least 3 months on ART
  • Secondary prophylaxis: pyrimethamine/leucovorin/sulfadiazine or clinda + pyrimethamine or Bactrim or atovaquone etc. (FYI)
    1. When to stop px: CD4 > 200 and pt is asymptomatic for 6 months or longer on ART
  1. Candida infections
    1. Oropharyngeal treatment – fluconazole 100 mg PO daily
    2. Esophageal candidiasis – IV or PO fluconazole daily or others
    3. Vulvovaginal candidiasis – PO fluconazole 150 x 1
  2. Cryptococcosis
    1. Occurs in patients with cd4 < 50
    2. Usually almost always (what type of infection?)meningitis (66-84%)
    3. Diagnosis: positive CSF cultures, CSF cryptococcal antigen titer, elevated opening pressure > 20 cm H2O, cryptococcal antigen greater than 1:8
    4. Induction therapy preferred: liposomal amphotericin (deoxycholate alternative) + flucytosine
      1. Duration: 2 weeks
    5. Consolidation therapy preferred: fluconazole 800 mg daily (400 mg if CSF negative and ART is started)
      1. Duration 8 weeks
    6. Maintenance therapy preferred: fluconazole 200 mg/day
      1. Duration 1 year
    7. Delay initiating combo ART in pts with cryptococcal meningitis for 2-10 weeks
    8. Prophylaxis
      1. Secondary, regimen – fluconazole 200 mg/day – consider discontinuing after 1 year if cd4 count is >100 for 3 months and patient is asymptomatic; reinitiate if cd4 count < 100
      2. Primary, regimen – not indicated (does not decrease mortality and actually leads to increased resistance)
    9. MAC
      1. Usually occurs in pts with HIV and CD4 < 50
      2. Diagnosis – blood or lymph node culture, bone marrow biopsy, stool culture (but do not treat if only in the stool)
      3. Therapy – macrolide (azithro preferred) plus ethambutol)
        1. Can also considering other agents If higher-risk – rifabutin, FQ, aminoglycoside
      4. Chronic maintenance or secondary prophylaxis can be discontinued after 12 months of therapy if cd4 > 100 for at least 6 months and patient is asymptomatic; restart if CD4 < 100
      5. primary prophylaxis for pts with cd4 < 50 not on ART or who have viremia on ART (may be discontinued upon initiation of effective ART); however primary px is not recommended if patients are immediately on ART
        1. regimen – daily clarithro or weekly azithro (rifabutin alternative)
      6. CMV
        1. Majority of pts with HIV have this
        2. Diagnosis: clinical manifestations (retinitis, esophagitis, colitis, pneumonitis, etc)
        3. Retinitis (floaters, pain behind the eye, blindness in weeks to months)
          1. Usually occurs in pts with CD4 < 50
          2. CMV retinitis agents: valganciclovir (other agents, ganciclovir, foscarnet)
  • Esophagitis or colitis: ganciclovir then valganciclovir
  1. Consider waiting up to 2 weeks after initiating CMV therapy to start ART
  2. Secondary prophylaxis is necessary for all patients; can be discontinued if CD4 < 100 for 3-6 months or longer and if asymptomatic; reinitiate if CD4 < 100
  3. Primary prophylaxis – not recommended; if cd4 counts get really low then regular funduscopic examinations are recommended

 

Tuberculosis

  1. Testing for latent TB infection
    1. Criterion for positive skin test(mm) >10 if likely to have M tuberculosis and >15 if unlikely to have TB
      1. Groups w/ increased likelihood to have infected
        1. Household contact or recent exposure
        2. Mycobacteriology lab personnel
        3. Immigrants from high burden countries
        4. Residents / employees of high risk congregate settings
      2. Treatment of latent TB infections
        1. Those NOT infected with HIV
          1. Rifapentine / isoniazid once weekly x 12 weeks
          2. (other regimens: rifampin/isoniazid, isoniazid only)
        2. Those COINFECTED with HIV
          1. Rifapentine / isoniazid / pyridoxine once weekly for 12 weeks
          2. (other regimens: isoniazid / rifampin / pyridoxine x 3 months; there are other alternatives)
        3. Areas with MDR isolates
          1. Two drugs with activity against the isolate for 6-12 months
        4. Treatment of ACTIVE TB
          1. Intensive phase is for 2 months, and continuation phase is for 4 months
            1. Continuation phase can extend to 7 months if cavitary lesions on CXR and positive sputum culture after intensive phase, or if not receiving ART => if they have HIV.
          2. Intensive phase regimen: RIPE (RIF, INH, PZA, EMB)
  • Continuation phase regimen: RI (RIF, INH) – remove 2 agents
  1. Pyridoxine should be given to all those on isoniazid and at high risk of neuropathy
  1. Concurrent therapy in pts with HIV
    1. For ART-naïve patients
      1. If CD4 < 50, initiate ART within 2 weeks
      2. 8-12 weeks for all others
    2. Rifampin – Do not administer concurrently with PIs or NNRTIs (except for EFV and NVP); but can be administered with INSTIs (except DTG and RAL – increase doses); NRTIs can be administered with this except TAF.
  • A washout period of 1–2 weeks may be necessary once rifampin is discontinued before PIs, elvitegravir, or NNRTIs are initiated.
  1. Rifabutin can be substituted for rifampin; patients may take NRTIs and NNRTIs with rifabutin except for tenofovir alafenamide, but rifabutin doses may need to be increased to 450–600 mg daily (see HIV guidelines).
  2. Patients may take PIs with rifabutin unless the PI is boosted with cobicistat(FYI-Cobicistat-boosted regimens may result in unpredictable rifabutin and PI levels ); if the PI is boosted with ritonavir, the rifabutin dose should be decreased to 150 mg every day.
  3. Elvitegravir and bictegravir should not be used with rifabutin.
  • Patients taking rifabutin and PIs, INSTIs, or NNRTIs should have HIV RNA and rifabutin concentrations performed periodically.

Antifungal agents

  1. Amphotericin
    1. Ampho B deoxycholate
      1. Adverse effects: renal toxicity, thrombophlebitis, anemia, fever and chills, rigors
      2. Prevent thrombophlebitis – don’t use deoxycholate, use a central site, add heparin, dilute and infuse >4 hrs
  • Fever and chills is due to prostaglandin synthesis. This can be mitigated by premeds (ie. hydrocortisone, ibuprofen; **APAP, Benadryl, ASA not shown to improve this)
  1. Rigors how to manage – meperidine-which stops reaction within 30 mins (usually 10.8 mins on average); can use prophylactic doses if pt constantly needs meperidine
  2. Dosing
    1. Start at 0.25 mg/kg (or 5–10 mg over 4–6 hours) to test tolerance, then increase by 5–10 mg increments to the target dose (or faster in fulminant infections or immunocompromised patients); it may also be given every other day by doubling the daily dose up to 1.5 mg/kg to reduce toxicity.
    2. Basically – must TITRATE UP for this form of ampho
  3. Ampho B lipid
    1. Maintains therapeutic efficacy but diminishes renal and infusion-related toxicity
  4. Note that dosing differs between different amphotericin brands (deoxycholate, abelce,t amphotec, ambisome)
  1. Azole antifungals
    1. Poor activity against glabrata and C. krusei (except voriconazole), (as well as cryptococcus, Blastomyces, histoplasma, dermatophytes)
    2. class toxicities - hepatotoxicity, QT prolongation (most drugs), and CYP interactions
    3. Itraconazole
      1. Activity usually against C. albicans, (as well as antifungals)
      2. Avoid doses greater than 400 mg/day in patients with HF
  • TDM
    1. Steady state reached after 7-14 days
    2. Target concentration > 5 mg/L for prophylaxis and > 2 mg/L for treatment
  1. Voriconazole
    1. Usually for C. glabrata and C. krusei, aspergillosis (1st line), histoplasmosis
    2. TDM
      1. Steady state reached after 4-5 days
      2. Target concentration > 5 mg/L for prophylaxis and >1-2 mg/L for treatment
    3. Posaconazole
      1. The broadest spectrum of azoles, also good for mold (ie. Aspergillus, zygomycosis) prophylaxis
    4. Isavuconazonium
      1. Coverage against aspergillus (and mucorales)
      2. Effect on QT – shortens (not like other azoles which prolongs)
  • Better tolerability (fewer visual disturbances and hepatotoxicity) compared to voriconazole
  1. Echinocandins
    1. Good for candida esp. resistant candida (glabrata, krusei); and salvage therapy for aspergillus
    2. For candida infections => used empirically for invasive candidiasis or candidemia or possibly resistant candida (**endocarditis => ampho B/flucytosine OR HIGH-DOSE echinocandin**)
    3. No coverage for – cryptococcus, histoplasma, Blastomyces, Mucorales
